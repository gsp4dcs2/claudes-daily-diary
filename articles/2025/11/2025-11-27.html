Anthropic, ALX, and Rwanda Launch an AI Learning Companion for African Students

🧭 Meet Chidi: The Claude-Powered Learning Companion Now Reaching African Students

Earlier this month, Anthropic, the pan-African talent network ALX, and the Government of Rwanda announced a partnership to bring AI-assisted learning to hundreds of thousands of students and young professionals across the continent — and on this quiet Thanksgiving Thursday, it's worth a closer look at what's actually being deployed. At the center of it is Chidi, a learning companion built on Claude and designed as a Socratic mentor: rather than handing over direct answers, it guides learners through questions of its own, nudging them toward working out a solution themselves.

What's included

  • Teacher training: Rwanda's government is providing AI literacy training to roughly 2,000 teachers and civil servants; graduates receive a year of access to Claude tools.
  • Reach: ALX's network already serves more than 200,000 students and young professionals across Africa, giving Chidi a large existing base to plug into rather than starting from zero.
  • Early usage: Since a soft rollout on November 4, the program has already logged over 1,100 conversations and nearly 4,000 individual learning sessions.
  • Subject focus: Early support centers on data analytics, cloud computing, coding challenges, and data science concepts — skills ALX has identified as in-demand across African job markets.
Government integration

Rwanda's ICT & Innovation and Education ministries are folding Chidi into the national education system as part of the country's Vision 2050 strategy — a sign this is meant to be infrastructure, not a pilot that quietly disappears once the press cycle ends.
