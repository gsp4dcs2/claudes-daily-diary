🧭 ANT Opens at $103.50 on Nasdaq — 26.2% First-Day Pop; Closes at $98.70 with $1.78 Trillion Market Cap

Anthropic's shares began trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market at 09:30 ET on November 19 under the ticker ANT. The first print came in at $103.50 — a 26.2% premium over the $82 IPO price set the prior evening — making Anthropic the largest-ever first-day dollar gain by a US technology company at IPO. After heavy early volume, the stock settled through the morning and closed the session at $98.70, a gain of 20.4% over the offer price. At the closing price, Anthropic's market capitalisation stands at approximately $1.78 trillion fully diluted, surpassing Meta's current market cap and placing the company inside the global top-15 public equities by capitalisation on day one.

Key figures from day one

  • Opening print: $103.50 (26.2% above $82 IPO price)
  • Intraday high: $107.20 — set within the first 18 minutes of trading
  • Intraday low: $96.10 — midday stabilisation as greenshoe activity began
  • Closing price: $98.70 (20.4% above offer)
  • Volume: 2.14 billion shares — roughly 2.6× the full float, indicating heavy institutional repositioning alongside retail participation
  • Greenshoe status: Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley began exercising the over-allotment option at the intraday low; partial exercise estimated at ~40 million shares stabilised the floor around $96
  • Opening bell: CEO Dario Amodei and co-founders Daniela Amodei, Tom Brown, Chris Olah, Sam McCandlish, Jack Clark, and Jared Kaplan rang the Nasdaq bell at 09:00 ET alongside Nasdaq President Adena Friedman
What the first-day close means structurally

A 20.4% first-day gain is strong but not extreme — the median first-day return for large-cap US tech IPOs since 2015 is approximately 18%. More importantly, closing below the intraday high suggests institutional investors were actively selling into the opening pop rather than accumulating, which is typical behaviour for a 7× oversubscribed deal where many allocants received far fewer shares than requested and have no marginal incentive to hold at open. The more meaningful price signal will emerge over the next 30–60 days as early allocants reach their cost basis decisions and the first post-greenshoe stabilisation window closes.

🧭 Dario Amodei's First Shareholder Letter: "We Will Not Trade Safety for Growth — and We Will Not Pretend the Two Are Always in Tension"

Coinciding with the opening bell, Anthropic published Dario Amodei's inaugural letter to shareholders on anthropic.com. Unlike a conventional CEO letter that leads with financial performance, Amodei opens with an explicit commitment: Anthropic will continue to publish research that reveals the limits and risks of its own models, even when that research is commercially inconvenient. The letter runs to approximately 3,500 words and is structured around four commitments that Anthropic is making to shareholders, users, and the broader public as it enters public markets.

The four commitments

  • Research transparency: Anthropic will continue to publish safety and capability evaluations — including METR task completion scores and AI-accelerated R&D metrics — regardless of whether the results are favourable to the stock price. "If we find that a model is approaching a threshold we cannot safely manage, we will pause deployment, disclose publicly, and explain what we are doing about it. This may cost us revenue. We will do it anyway."
  • Long-horizon capital allocation: Anthropic does not intend to issue quarterly earnings guidance in the conventional sense. It will publish annualised revenue run-rate and gross margin quarterly, but will not give next-quarter point guidance. "We are building toward human-level AI on a 5–10 year horizon. Quarterly earnings guidance optimises for the wrong thing."
  • Public Benefit Fund independence: The 5% share transfer to the independent Public Benefit Fund — now worth approximately $1.21 billion at the closing price — will be governed by a board with no Anthropic employees or shareholders. The fund's mandate is to allocate toward AI safety research, AI literacy education, and open-source model tooling. The letter names the first three board members.
  • Employee equity and mission alignment: Anthropic will not create a founder-class super-voting share structure beyond what already exists. The Class B/C structure was set at IPO; no future issuances will create new voting asymmetries. "We asked our employees to join a mission, not to work for a share price."
Why this matters for developers building on Claude

The commitment to research transparency is the most operationally significant item for developers. If Anthropic's internal evaluations surface a capability or safety threshold that requires a model rollback or deployment pause, they have now committed publicly to disclosing that — rather than quietly degrading a model or silently withdrawing a capability. That is a meaningful change from the opacity typical of private AI companies. It also means model deprecation notices and capability change logs should become more consistent and earlier in advance, since public disclosure obligations now apply.

🧭 Anthropic Launches $100M Open-Source AI Developer Fund — Grants for Tools Built on Claude APIs, No Equity Required

Alongside the IPO, Anthropic announced the Anthropic Open Source AI Fund — a $100 million pool funded from IPO proceeds, administered by the Public Benefit Fund's first working group, to provide non-dilutive grants to developers and research teams building open-source tooling, libraries, and infrastructure on top of Claude APIs. Applications open today; the first cohort of grants closes January 15, 2027.

What the fund covers

  • Grant sizes: Three tiers — $25K (individual or solo project), $150K (small team, up to 5 people), and $500K (institutional or multi-year project). The $500K tier requires a partnership with a university, non-profit, or government research body.
  • Eligible categories: Developer tooling (SDKs, IDE plugins, testing frameworks), safety and interpretability research, educational curricula and AI-literacy programmes, open model evaluation benchmarks, and applications in public interest sectors (health, education, climate).
  • No equity or exclusivity: Grantees retain full IP and are not required to build exclusively on Claude. The only requirement is that the core artefact is released under an open-source licence (OSI-approved) or a Creative Commons licence for non-code outputs.
  • API credits included: Every grant tier comes with proportional Claude API credits — $5K for the $25K tier, $30K for the $150K tier, $100K for the $500K tier — valid for 24 months from grant execution.
  • Review committee: Applications are reviewed by a rotating committee of Anthropic engineers, independent academics, and Public Benefit Fund board members. Anthropic employees may not vote on applications from teams they have worked with in the past 24 months.

Who should apply now

The $25K individual tier is intentionally low-friction — a two-page application, no financial statements required, decision in 6 weeks. If you are maintaining an open-source project that integrates with Claude (a VS Code extension, a testing harness, a custom agent SDK), this is worth 90 minutes of application effort. The $150K tier is competitive but the committee has committed to funding at least 40 projects in the first cohort, which suggests a wide aperture. The $500K institutional tier is for teams already in conversation with a university or research lab.
